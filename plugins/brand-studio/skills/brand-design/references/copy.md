# Copy without AI slop

Every visible string is design content. Write it last, from verified facts, then run the audit below on each string: headline, subline, labels, captions, alt text, buttons and footer. A string that fails one check gets rewritten, not softened.

## What a line should be

- A plain sentence with a subject, a verb and a checkable fact: who, what, when, how many. "Mantegna painted a Chinese porcelain cup around 1500." beats "Cobalt belonged to the cup first."
- One job per string. The headline says what this is; the subline adds the one fact that makes it specific; the button names the next step.
- Buttons describe the action and its result ("See the three paintings", "Book a table for Saturday"). Cut "Get started", "Learn more", "Start with the gift".
- Numbers, names, dates and places over adjectives. If the fact is unknown, leave the slot empty or ask; never invent it.

## Word budget

- Hero: a headline of about 5 words, one supporting line, two buttons.
- Section heads: 3-6 words. Prefer a caption under a visual to a paragraph beside it.
- A paragraph is a sign the visual is not showing the point. Fix the visual first.

## Slop signals: rewrite on sight

Line shapes
- Aphorism or pull-quote headline: a short, rhythmic line that sounds wise and states nothing checkable ("Cobalt belonged to the cup first.", "Same cup. A closer look.").
- Setup lines: "Before X, …", "It all started with…", "Here's where it gets interesting", "What if…".
- Negative parallelism: "not X, but Y", "not just X, it's Y", "X. Ours does." reversals.
- Staccato clusters: three short fragments for fake tension ("One cup. One counter. One light.").
- Reflexive rule of three: triplets where the content has two or four things; benefit stacks ("Fast, secure, reliable").
- Personification for drama: objects that "sit down", "remember", "belong", "wait".
- Closing lines that restate the page ("The cup is on the counter.").

Words
- Significance and legacy: stands as, testament, pivotal, timeless, enduring, legacy, five centuries later.
- Promotional and heritage: vibrant, rich, profound, nestled, boasts, curated, crafted, elevate, unlock, seamless, game-changer.
- "serves as" / "stands as" where "is" works; "leverage" for "use"; "craft" for "make".
- Filler intensifiers and hedges: truly, incredibly, arguably, worth noting.
- Vague attribution: "experts say", "research shows" without the source.

Page chrome
- Eyebrow label above every heading, or a product-name eyebrow above the hero.
- Em dashes as rhythm, mid-headline italics or colour on one word, Title Case, emoji bullets, bold as decoration.
- Mad-lib hero ("The AI-powered X for modern Y"), generic mission-statement subline, categories as features ("Analytics. Integrations.").
- Polished testimonials or reviews nobody said.

## Audit procedure

1. List every visible string in a scratch file.
2. For each, answer: what fact does it state, and could a competitor paste it unchanged? No fact, or yes, then rewrite.
3. Read the headline and subline aloud together. If they form a setup and a punchline, cut one.
4. Search the strings for the word list above and for "—", "not ", "just ", "Before ".
5. Keep the brand voice in word choice and rhythm; never at the cost of the fact.
